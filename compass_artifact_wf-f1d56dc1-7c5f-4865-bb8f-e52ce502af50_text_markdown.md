# Continuous Shape-Space Methods for Mouse USV Repertoire and "Syntax" Analysis

## TL;DR
- The strongest path forward is to lean fully into the registered 1-D contour as a *function/curve* and analyze it with **elastic functional data analysis (SRVF/Fisher–Rao elastic FPCA)** plus **soft-DTW/elastic-kernel** tooling; these give interpretable, warp-invariant "shape axes" by construction and are the principled generalization of the soft-DTW lever the user already validated (Realization 3).
- The "USVs are a continuum" finding is strongly corroborated by the field (Goffinet et al. 2021 eLife provides "strong evidence that mouse ultrasonic vocalizations do not cluster as is commonly believed"), so the user should stop treating K=20 as natural kinds and instead use **soft/continuous representations** (archetypal analysis, elastic FPCA scores) and **manifold-shape tests** (persistent homology, density-ridge/SCMS, intrinsic-dimension, PHATE) to characterize the blob rigorously.
- For "grammar," the user's skepticism is well-founded but should be stated carefully: use **continuous information-theoretic and dynamical tests** (KSG mutual information / transfer entropy on contour coordinates, RQA, SLDS) with **surrogate/permutation controls** that explicitly remove pitch/duration autocorrelation; and for cross-cohort comparison, **optimal transport / MMD on shape distributions with the cage axis controlled by stratification or domain-invariant projection**.

## Key Findings

The user's empirical work is largely consistent with the frontier literature. Three conclusions are robust: (1) the continuum finding matches Goffinet, Brudner, Mooney & Pearson (2021, eLife), whose VAE "provides strong evidence that mouse ultrasonic vocalizations do not cluster as is commonly believed" but rather "form a broad continuum." (2) The warping/elastic lever is exactly what the SRVF/Fisher–Rao functional-data tradition was built for — amplitude/phase separation. (3) The "grammar may be an artifact" worry is the kind of claim that continuous information theory plus surrogate testing is designed to adjudicate.

The most important new input from a deep dive into learned encoders: principled warp-invariant neural encoders **do** exist (DTAN, ResNet-TW, TTN, SrvfNet/SrvfRegNet, DeepFRC), but the honest empirical record is **not** a clean win over hand-crafted elastic registration on alignment quality — their decisive advantage is amortized inference and scalability, not fidelity. On the UCR benchmark, DTAN was "better or no worse" than hand-crafted soft-DTW on only 62% of datasets; Martinez et al. (2022) report DTAN, ResNet-TW and soft-DTW clustering in a comparable ~69–76% band against their closed-form baseline. This directly informs whether the user should revisit a learned encoder: it would not be expected to beat a strong elastic-FPCA baseline on a fixed 67k corpus.

## Details and Ranked Shortlist

### 1. Elastic functional PCA on the registered contour (SRVF / Fisher–Rao) — TOP RECOMMENDATION
**Core idea.** Treat each call as an open curve/function and analyze it under the elastic (Fisher–Rao) Riemannian metric via the square-root velocity function (SRVF), which converts the Fisher–Rao metric into a simple L² metric and makes distances invariant to reparameterization (warping). One then computes a Karcher/Fréchet mean, separates amplitude (shape) from phase (warp), and runs elastic functional PCA to get interpretable continuous shape axes plus a separate PCA on the warping functions (Tucker, Wu & Srivastava 2013; Srivastava & Klassen 2016; Srivastava, Wu, Kurtek, Klassen & Marron 2011).
**Citations.** Srivastava, Klassen, Joshi & Jermyn (2011, IEEE TPAMI); Srivastava, Wu, Kurtek, Klassen & Marron (2011, registration via Fisher–Rao); Tucker, Wu & Srivastava (2013, Comput. Stat. Data Anal., "Generative Models for Functional Data using Phase and Amplitude Separation"); Srivastava & Klassen (2016, *Functional and Shape Data Analysis*, Springer). Software: `fdasrvf` (R) and `fdasrsf` (Python).
**Why it fits.** This is the mathematically principled version of the user's hand-crafted pipeline: it operates on the 1-D contour, builds warp-invariance into the metric by construction, and the SRVF L² metric is exactly the elastic geodesic distance whose soft-DTW approximation already won for the user. It is invariant to reparameterization/time-position and (after mean subtraction) to absolute frequency, so capacity is spent on shape, not nuisance. Tucker et al. (2013) explicitly note that ignoring phase variability "leads to a loss of structure in the data and inefficiency in data models" — precisely the failure mode the user diagnosed in pixel VAEs.
**What it lets the user claim.** "Our shape axes are the principal modes of a warp-invariant elastic metric; PC1/PC2 are interpretable shape gradients (e.g., valley→flat→step), and we can quantitatively partition variance into amplitude (shape) vs phase (warp) components" — a claim impossible with Euclidean PCA on raw curves.
**Cost.** Low–moderate. `fdasrvf`/`fdasrsf` are mature; the DP alignment is O(T²) per pair but T=50 is tiny. Karcher mean + elastic FPCA on 67k curves is feasible (alignment to a common mean is O(N), not O(N²)). Main effort: porting registered curves into the package and choosing the elasticity parameter.

### 2. Soft-DTW barycenters, elastic kernels, and scalable warping tooling
**Core idea.** Generalize the validated soft-DTW lever: use soft-DTW as a differentiable discrepancy to compute **barycenters** (mean shapes) and to cluster under DTW geometry (Cuturi & Blondel 2017); use **Global Alignment Kernels (GAK)** to get a positive-definite elastic kernel for kernel-PCA/kernel-SVM/spectral methods (Cuturi, Vert, Birkenes & Matsui 2007; Cuturi 2011); and scale to 67k via landmarking, k-NN graphs, Nyström low-rank kernel approximation, and DTW lower bounds (LB_Keogh).
**Citations.** Cuturi & Blondel (2017, ICML, Soft-DTW); Blondel, Mensch & Vert (2021, AISTATS, soft-DTW divergences); Cuturi, Vert, Birkenes & Matsui (2007); Cuturi (2011, ICML, Fast Global Alignment Kernels); Keogh & Ratanamahatana (2005, LB_Keogh). Software: `tslearn`, `pysdtw`.
**Why it fits.** Warping invariance is the whole point, and GAK turns the user's best-performing distance into a PSD kernel, enabling kernel-PCA "shape axes" and MMD-based comparisons (see #6) that respect warping. The full 67k×67k matrix never needs materializing — Nyström and k-NN graphs use a few thousand landmark columns. Cuturi & Blondel (2017) report that soft-DTW barycenters improve nearest-centroid accuracy over DBA on 75% of UCR datasets.
**What it lets the user claim.** "Continuous shape coordinates and group comparisons built on a warp-invariant PSD kernel, scaled to the full corpus without ever computing the full pairwise matrix."
**Cost.** Moderate. `tslearn` has soft-DTW barycenters and GAK; Nyström is a few lines. Choosing γ and landmarks needs care.

### 3. Archetypal analysis (and simplex/NMF soft membership) for a graded repertoire
**Core idea.** Instead of hard K=20 labels, represent each call as a convex combination of a few extreme "archetype" shapes lying on the convex hull of the data (Cutler & Breiman 1994; Mørup & Hansen 2012). This directly matches the user's picture: a few extreme shape types (sharp step, deep valley, flat) with everything as a graded mixture in between.
**Citations.** Cutler & Breiman (1994, Technometrics); Mørup & Hansen (2012, Neurocomputing, "Archetypal analysis for machine learning and data mining"); kernel archetypal analysis (Mørup & Hansen) allows AA in the GAK/elastic kernel space. Software: `py_pcha`, `archetypes` (Python), `archetypes` (R).
**Why it fits.** Soft membership is the honest representation of a continuum — it provides interpretable extremes plus continuous weights, not artificial categories. Run on elastic-FPCA scores or in the GAK kernel space (kernel-AA), it inherits warp/time/frequency invariance from the upstream representation. Note the formal analogy to a graded-repertoire interpretation seen in other species: the wild boar study (Garcia et al. 2016) found "four call types…with the extremes representing discrete call types" but acoustically continuous intermediates — exactly the structure archetypal analysis encodes (archetypes = extremes, mixtures = intermediates).
**What it lets the user claim.** "USV shape is captured by N archetypes; each call is a graded mixture, and cohorts differ in *where on the simplex* they sit" — a continuum-respecting alternative to transition matrices over discrete types.
**Cost.** Low–moderate. AA is a small optimization; convergence/initialization (FurthestSum) needs attention. Choosing the number of archetypes is the main modeling decision.

### 4. Manifold-shape characterization: persistent homology + density ridges + intrinsic dimension + PHATE
**Core idea.** Rigorously test *what shape* the continuum is — filled blob vs 1-D curve/loop vs branching tree vs multi-modal ridge system — rather than asserting "one blob." Use **persistent homology** to detect loops/voids/components (with the caveat that naive PH is noise-sensitive in high dimensions; use spectral/kNN-graph PH); **subspace-constrained mean shift (SCMS)** to estimate density ridges/principal curves; **intrinsic-dimension estimation**; and **PHATE/diffusion maps** to visualize continuous progressions without shattering them into clusters (unlike t-SNE/UMAP, which Moon et al. show "shatter trajectories into clusters").
**Citations.** Edelsbrunner & Harer (persistent homology); Ozertem & Erdogmus (2011, JMLR, "Locally Defined Principal Curves and Surfaces"/SCMS); Genovese, Perone-Pacifico, Verdinelli & Wasserman (2014, nonparametric ridge estimation); Moon et al. (2019, Nat. Biotechnol., PHATE); Coifman & Lafon (2006, diffusion maps); spectral-PH for high-dimensional noisy data (NeurIPS 2024). Software: `ripser`/`giotto-tda`, `pyphate`, SCMS implementations.
**Why it fits.** These operate on the warp-invariant elastic coordinates (#1/#2), so the topology being tested is shape topology, not pitch/time topology. PHATE specifically preserves continuous gradients, matching the "smooth morph" picture.
**What it lets the user claim.** "The shape manifold is statistically a [single contractible blob / 1-D curve / has a detached noisy component]; intrinsic dimension is ~d; the small oscillatory pocket is topologically distinct (persistent H₀ component)." This converts a qualitative UMAP impression into a defensible claim.
**Cost.** Moderate. TDA on 67k points needs subsampling/witness complexes; PHATE and SCMS scale with landmarking.

### 5. Continuous sequential structure without discretization: KSG MI / transfer entropy + RQA + SLDS, with surrogate controls
**Core idea.** Ask whether shape *sequences* carry predictive structure beyond pitch/duration autocorrelation, without ever discretizing. (a) Estimate continuous mutual information / transfer entropy between successive calls' shape coordinates using the **Kraskov–Stögbauer–Grassberger (KSG)** kNN estimator; condition on (or partial out) pitch/duration to isolate shape-sequence information. (b) Use **recurrence quantification analysis (RQA)** (determinism, laminarity) on the latent trajectory. (c) Fit **switching linear dynamical systems (SLDS/rSLDS)** as continuous-state sequence models. Crucially, validate against **surrogate/permutation tests** (shuffle within bout, circular shifts, phase-randomized surrogates) to separate genuine sequence structure from autocorrelation and bout-onset effects.
**Citations.** Kraskov, Stögbauer & Grassberger (2004, Phys. Rev. E, "Estimating mutual information"); Schreiber (2000, transfer entropy); Lizier (2014, JIDT toolkit); Zbilut & Webber / Marwan et al. (RQA); Linderman et al. (2017, AISTATS, recurrent SLDS). Software: JIDT, IDTxl, `pyRQA`, `ssm` (Linderman lab).
**Why it fits.** All operate on continuous coordinates — no alphabet required, so they sidestep the discretization that the user showed inflates/deflates transition MI. Conditioning and surrogates directly test the user's Realization 4 (that "grammar" was pitch/duration autocorrelation). The broad bout-MI plateau (~0.15–1.0 s) can itself be a reported result, with ~0.25 s as a sensitivity-tested choice.
**What it lets the user claim.** "After conditioning on pitch and duration and against bout-matched surrogates, shape-to-shape transfer entropy is [indistinguishable from / exceeds] the autocorrelation null" — a direct, quantitative resolution of the grammar-artifact question.
**Cost.** Moderate. KSG/TE estimators are sensitive to dimension and k; needs careful surrogate design. SLDS fitting is more involved but `ssm` is available.

### 6. Confound-robust repertoire comparison: optimal transport / MMD with cage-axis control
**Core idea.** Compare *distributions* of shape (not point estimates) between cohorts/individuals using **Wasserstein/optimal-transport distances** (Peyré & Cuturi 2019; entropic Sinkhorn for scalability) and/or **maximum mean discrepancy (MMD)** with a characteristic (or GAK elastic) kernel (Gretton et al. 2012). Control the dominant "cage"/recording-environment axis by (a) computing comparisons *within* cage strata, (b) conditioning/regressing out cage via mixed-effects models on continuous coordinates, or (c) domain-invariant projection that removes the cage direction before comparison.
**Citations.** Peyré & Cuturi (2019, *Computational Optimal Transport*); Cuturi (2013, Sinkhorn distances); Gretton, Borgwardt, Rasch, Schölkopf & Smola (2012, JMLR, "A Kernel Two-Sample Test"); MMD subsampling/reference-set bounds (Cloninger 2018). Software: `POT` (Python Optimal Transport), `geomloss` (Sinkhorn divergences), MMD implementations.
**Why it fits.** OT and MMD compare full continuous distributions, ideal for a graded repertoire, and (with an elastic/GAK ground metric) the comparison is in warp-invariant shape space. The cage-stratification requirement maps directly onto Realization 5 (cage dominates geometry; between-pair distances are a noise floor).
**What it lets the user claim.** "Within matched recording environments, wild pair X's shape distribution differs from lab partner-swap distribution Y by Wasserstein distance W (permutation p<...), and this exceeds the cross-cage noise floor" — a confound-honest cross-cohort claim.
**Cost.** Moderate. `POT`/`geomloss` are mature; Sinkhorn is GPU-friendly. Main effort is the cage-control design and permutation inference.

### 7. (Conditional) Warp-invariant *learned* encoders on the 1-D contour — only if a specific scalability/amortization need arises
**Core idea.** If a learned encoder is still wanted, the principled family is one that **internalizes warping** rather than treating it as nuisance: **Diffeomorphic Temporal Alignment Nets (DTAN)** and its regularization-free sequel (CPAB diffeomorphic warps as temporal-transformer layers), **ResNet-TW** (LDDMM-style flow), **Temporal Transformer Networks (TTN)** (jointly invariant+discriminative warps), and SRVF-native networks **SrvfNet/SrvfRegNet** and the end-to-end **DeepFRC** (class-aware elastic warping + classification with a theoretical alignment↔generalization bound). Soft-DTW or the SRVF elastic metric can serve as a differentiable reconstruction loss; **DecDTW** provides an exact differentiable DTW layer.
**Citations.** Weber, Eyal, Detlefsen, Shriki & Freifeld (2019, NeurIPS, DTAN); Shapira Weber & Freifeld (2023, ICML, regularization-free DTAN); Huang et al. (2021, ResNet-TW, arXiv:2106.11911); Lohit, Wang & Turaga (2019, CVPR, TTN); Freifeld et al. (2017, IEEE TPAMI, CPAB); Martinez, Viles & Olaizola (2022, ICML, closed-form CPAB); Nunez, Lizarraga & Joshi (2021, CVPRW, SrvfNet); Chen & Srivastava (2021, CVPRW, SrvfRegNet); Jiang, Hu, Li & Zeng (2025, DeepFRC, arXiv:2501.18116); Xu, Garg, Milford & Gould (2023, ICLR, DecDTW); Cuturi & Blondel (2017, soft-DTW loss).
**Why it fits / the honest caveat.** These are the *only* learned encoders that respect the time/warp-invariance desideratum by architecture. BUT the empirical record is mixed and does not justify abandoning #1. In the DTAN nearest-centroid experiment (Weber et al. 2019), DTAN was "better or no worse" than Euclidean on 93% of UCR datasets and DBA on 77%, but versus hand-crafted soft-DTW on only **62%** — i.e., soft-DTW was competitive or better on ~38%. Martinez et al. (2022) report their closed-form method beat or tied Euclidean (94%), DBA (77%), SoftDTW (69%), DTAN (76%), and ResNet-TW (70%), placing **DTAN, ResNet-TW and soft-DTW in a comparable ~69–76% band**. Learned methods win on **amortized inference and scalability**, not alignment fidelity. For small registered 1-D datasets the guidance conflicts: TTN reports learned warps help most when data is small, while Hartman et al. argue learning a full warp map needs more data than a scalar elastic distance.
**What it lets the user claim.** Realistically: "An amortized warp-invariant encoder reproduces elastic-FPCA structure at constant inference cost" — useful if they need to encode millions of future calls cheaply. It would NOT be expected to beat #1 on a fixed 67k corpus.
**Cost.** High (training, tuning, validation against #1 baseline). Recommended only as a follow-on, with elastic FPCA as the benchmark to beat.

## On the transformer + VQ-VAE hunch
The user and PI's intuition about transformer→VQ-VAE is worth addressing directly. VQ-VAE (van den Oord, Vinyals & Kavukcuoglu 2017) learns a *discrete* codebook, which is in direct tension with the continuum finding — vector quantization re-imposes exactly the discrete-types framing the user's data argues against. Two recent bioacoustics efforts illustrate the tradeoff: TweetyBERT (self-supervised transformer on birdsong, 2025) learns syllable/phrase structure via masked prediction without VQ, and Sarkar & Magimai-Doss (2025, NeurIPS workshop) tokenize animal calls via VQ/Gumbel-VQ of HuBERT embeddings. While their VQ token sequences *can* discriminate call-types and callers, VQ "drops the performance across datasets by ∼26–39% … when compared to stats-pooling the same features and then classifying with a linear layer," and Gumbel-VQ performed "near chance-level, indicating potential codebook collapse." That is concrete evidence that forcing discreteness *hurts* relative to keeping continuous features. Principled version: if a transformer is used, prefer it on **continuous** contour coordinates (masked-prediction / sequence modeling over elastic-FPCA score sequences) rather than forcing VQ; reserve VQ only if the goal is an explicit symbolic inventory, and then test whether the codebook collapses to a continuum (codes that interpolate) rather than discrete kinds. The user's suspicion that their own VQ-VAE implementation was flawed is plausible, but even a correct one would re-introduce the discreteness problem and risk the same codebook-collapse and performance-drop seen by Sarkar & Magimai-Doss.

## Flags: where the literature CONTRADICTS the user's realizations
- **Discrete repertoires ARE well-justified in some species/contexts.** Many primate and other systems are genuinely discrete or mixed: discrete call types are documented (e.g., Diana/Campbell's monkey alarm calls; Zuberbühler), while others are graded (Fischer & Hammerschmidt). The wild boar study (Garcia et al. 2016, Ethology 122:329) classified 616 calls into "four call types: grunts, grunt-squeals, squeals, and trumpets" with 80.2% agreement with perceptual classification, while still finding "evidence supporting a graded interpretation of some wild boar vocalizations as acoustically continuous, with the extremes representing discrete call types." So "graded" is not universal; mouse USVs sit toward the graded end, but the field treats this as species- and context-specific.
- **Mouse USVs DO show non-random sequential transitions in multiple studies.** Holy & Guo (2005), Chabout et al. (2015), Castellucci et al. (2018), and Hertz et al. (2020, Commun. Biol.) all report non-random syllable transitions and behaviorally/strain-modulated sequence statistics. Chabout et al. (2015, n=12 B6D2F1/J males) explicitly rejected the null that "the probability of a mouse using syllable k…does not depend on the previously used syllable j," concluding "male mice modify their syntax, including specific sequences, length of sequence, repertoire composition…according to stimulus and social context." The user's finding that *shape-alphabet* transition MI collapses does not by itself prove there is *no* sequence structure — it shows the *discrete-shape* alphabet carries little, while pitch/duration/timing may carry real, behaviorally-relevant structure (Hertz et al. use temporal structure to improve labeling). The honest framing: the *shape* grammar may be weak, not that USV sequencing is structureless.
- **Learned encoders DID beat hand-crafted features in the canonical mouse-USV paper.** Goffinet et al. (2021) report their VAE "outperforms handpicked features on a variety of common analysis tasks." This is a real counterweight to Realization 2 — but note their VAE operated on spectrograms for *general* acoustic features, not on the user's already-registered 1-D shape contour. The user's seven failures are specifically about beating *hand-crafted registration of the contour*, a much stronger baseline than generic handpicked features. So the two findings are compatible: VAEs beat naive features but not an invariance-engineered representation.
- **VQ tokenization shows some signal for classification.** Sarkar & Magimai-Doss (2025) show VQ token sequences do discriminate call types/callers above chance — so discreteness is not useless for *classification*, even though it both misrepresents the underlying geometry and underperforms continuous pooling.

## Recommendations: IF I COULD ONLY RUN THREE THINGS NEXT (ordered, truth-optimized)
1. **Elastic FPCA (SRVF/Fisher–Rao) on the registered contours via `fdasrvf`/`fdasrsf`.** This is the single highest-value move: it generalizes the validated soft-DTW lever into a full statistical framework, yields interpretable warp-invariant shape axes, and explicitly partitions amplitude (shape) vs phase (warp) variance. Benchmark it against the existing hand-crafted pipeline using the same leave-one-out kNN retrieval-purity metric with bootstrap CIs. *Threshold to change course:* if elastic FPCA does not beat Euclidean PCA on retrieval purity, the elasticity parameter or registration is mis-set and should be retuned before proceeding.
2. **Continuous shape-sequence test: KSG transfer entropy on elastic-FPCA scores, conditioned on pitch/duration, against bout-matched surrogates.** This directly adjudicates the grammar-artifact question with no discretization. Report shape-TE vs the autocorrelation/surrogate null with CIs. *Threshold:* if conditioned shape-TE collapses to the surrogate null, the user can make the strong, defensible claim that USV "syntax" is shape-trivial and reflects pitch/duration/timing autocorrelation; if it exceeds the null, the shape grammar is real and worth modeling with SLDS.
3. **Manifold-shape test: persistent homology + SCMS density ridges + intrinsic dimension on the elastic coordinates.** This converts "one connected blob with a detached noisy pocket" from a UMAP impression into a quantitative topological claim, and tests whether the continuum is a filled blob vs a 1-D curve/branching structure. *Threshold:* a persistent H₀ component confirms the detached oscillatory pocket; non-trivial H₁ would indicate a loop (e.g., a cyclic shape progression) worth reporting.

Cross-cohort OT/MMD comparison (#6) is the natural fourth step once the shape representation and cage-control design are settled.

## Caveats
- Several cited 2025–2026 items (DTAN journal extension arXiv:2502.06591; DeepFRC arXiv:2501.18116; and related preprints) are not yet peer-reviewed; their headline superiority claims are authors' own reported results.
- "Invariance" from mean-pitch subtraction and resampling is invariance to *global* pitch/duration; it does not remove *within-call* covariation between shape and pitch that may be biologically real — elastic methods analyze the residual shape, which is the intended target but discards any shape–pitch coupling.
- KSG TE/MI estimates are sensitive to embedding dimension, k, and sample size; surrogate design (within-bout shuffles vs circular shifts vs phase randomization) materially affects conclusions and should be fixed in advance.
- The continuum-vs-discrete question is, as Goffinet et al. note, "in principle…impossible to answer definitively because, without the benefit of ground truth labels, clustering is an unsupervised classification task." Mouse *perception* of these calls (whether mice categorize a continuum, as the BootSnap authors raise) is an open empirical question that no acoustic analysis alone can settle.
- Topology on finite samples reflects density and sampling, not just the true manifold; PH results must be cross-validated against subsampling and noise models.
