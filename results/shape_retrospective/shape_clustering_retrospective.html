What we could have done better: unsupervised shape clustering of USVs

Goal — "finally get an unsupervised algorithm to cluster USVs that are geometrically close: chevron with chevron, jump with jump, flat with flat, invariant to absolute pitch and time."  ·  2026-06-02  ·  doc review + 35-agent verification workflow + fresh empirical probes, scored against the real 204-row human gold set (not the heuristic).

Bottom line. The single biggest thing we could have done better was measure honestly. The whole multi-month bake-off was graded by shape η² against the chevron_valley heuristic — a rule computed from the very ridge that registration's KMeans optimizes (doubly circular) — while a 204-row human-labeled gold set sat unused in the repo. When you finally score against the humans, the celebrated registration "win" (η² 0.58–0.75) is far more modest: it groups chevron and flat at ~2–3× base rate, but jump and complex sit at chance, and the heuristic everyone optimized agrees with humans at only precision 0.30 / recall 0.56. The misses were a measurement failure and one untried classical method family — not "we should have trained the encoder harder" (that is genuinely dead).

1 · The paradox — the goal was "met" by preprocessing, but only on a circular metric

The shipped winner is registration → KMeans (models/shape_kmeans/k20.joblib, rig): take the 1-D peak-frequency ridge F(t), subtract its mean (kill pitch), resample the active span to 50 points (kill duration/position), KMeans(20). It is pure DSP normalization — the invariances the user asked for are hard-coded into preprocessing; KMeans does what a human could do with a ruler. It scores shape η² 0.58–0.75 and beat every learned encoder:

AttemptSubstrateshape η²Verdict
Registration → KMeans1-D registered ridge0.58–0.75SHIPPED
M9 — 1-D contrastive (NT-Xent + elastic-warp aug)registered ridge0.34best learned, lost
Production contour-VAE K202-D image0.12pitch/dur sorter
B+A hybrid · denoised retrain · Pathway-B · M102-D image0.105 → 0.009KILL ×4

But shape η² is doubly circular. It is defined as 1 − within_SS/total_SS on the registered ridge — the exact vectors registration's KMeans minimizes variance over — and the chevron label it's checked against (chevron_valley) is itself a >2 kHz argmax/argmin rule on that same ridge. So the metric is "KMeans scored against a function of its own input." It structurally cannot credit any method that reorganizes the space, and it inflates registration's apparent dominance. Sources: archive/cleaning_legacy/stack3/scripts/shape_registered_clustering.py:129; docs/handoffs/2026-06-02_shape-vae-family-CLOSED.md.

data/manual_shape_labels.csv — 204 rows, 12-class human shape vocabulary (Chevron, Reverse Chevron, Up-/Down-FM, Step up/down, Two-/Multi-steps, Flat, Complex, Short, Noise), built by scripts/labeling/hand_label_200.py — existed and was never used to score registration. Honest reading. On real human labels, the registered ridge captures chevron (~2.6×) and flat (~2×) above base rate — a real but modest signal. Jump and complex are at or below chance for every representation, including the learned encoder. SRVF is marginally best for chevron (0.39). The "shape clustering solved" story is an artifact of the circular metric; against humans it is partially solved (chevron/flat) and unsolved (jump/complex).

Uncertainty (important). N is tiny and imbalanced — only 4 true "Chevron" (25 in the chevron family), all from lab cohort 131204 (wild cohorts unlabeled). These estimates are directional: the chevron number swung 0.16↔0.36 depending only on a det-index join offset, so treat ±0.1 as plausible. The robust claims are the rank (chevron/flat > jump/complex) and the heuristic audit below — not any single decimal.

3 · The metric the whole project optimized was a blurry proxy

Auditing chevron_valley (the bake-off's de-facto target) against the human chevron family: precision 0.30, recall 0.56 (14 true / 32 false-positive / 11 missed). So roughly two-thirds of what the heuristic called "chevron" the humans did not, and it missed nearly half the real ones. Every η² number, every kill decision, every "learned encoder failed at chevron" verdict was computed against this. Optimizing it hard was optimizing a smeared target — which is exactly why a method could win η² (registration) without actually grouping human-recognizable chevrons much better than 2–3× chance.

4 · What we could have done better — ranked (verification workflow, 22/26 levers survived)

Tier A — high-confidence, genuinely untried, plausibly competitive

A1 · lead leverTrue elastic shape analysis (SRVF / Fisher-Rao geodesic with per-pair warp alignment), done correctly. Registration removes only a global pitch shift and one global time-resample — it never aligns internal landmarks, so a chevron with its apex at t=0.3 and one at t=0.6 stay distant and KMeans splits the same shape. fdasrsf.kmeans_align (or a pairwise geodesic-distance matrix → k-medoids) optimizes a warping function per pair — the one operation registration lacks. The "SRVF" run so far (rig_R1_true_ridges.py:111) is just sign(dF)·√|dF| fed to Euclidean KMeans — no warp ever optimized. That crude version already edges registration on human chevron (0.39 vs 0.36), hinting at headroom.

✅ Tested this session (DTW / soft-DTW on the 182 labeled ridges, human-anchored kNN purity): elastic warp-alignment beats registration's Euclidean metric on every family — and most on the ones registration smears:

metricchevron (base 0.14)jump (base 0.35)flat (base 0.13)complex (base 0.07)
euclidean (registration)0.3640.3270.2740.117
DTW (elastic)0.4000.4490.2910.183
soft-DTW0.4040.4520.2870.183

Jump goes from at-chance (0.327, base 0.346) to clearly above it (0.45) — a step/jump is a discontinuity at a variable internal position; DTW warps the axis to align it, Euclidean cannot. This is the high-value outcome: the one untried classical lever measurably improves human-anchored clustering, especially where registration fails. Caveat: n=182, lab cohort only, ~25 chevrons — directional. Next: full-corpus soft-DTW k-means (tslearn, installed) or fdasrsf elastic alignment, scored on an expanded human set. Script: results/shape_retrospective/a1_elastic_test.py.

Tier B — make the problem well-posed (cheap, high-leverage; value is honesty, not a higher score)

B1Wire the 204-row human gold set in as the PRIMARY eval target (done in this report for the first time; should be the standing gate). Report NMI / ARI / kNN-purity vs humans + a heuristic-vs-human confusion matrix. Caveat: extend labels to wild cohorts and to ≥20 chevrons before trusting decimals.

B2Replace the η² gate with leave-one-out kNN retrieval purity vs humans, with a random-init/identity control — exactly what ROADMAP_SHAPE_INVARIANT_LATENT.md specified before the closure memo reverted to circular η².

B3Reframe the deliverable from "K=20 letters" to a navigable continuum shape-map. UMAP→HDBSCAN found a continuum (no crisp clusters); forcing 20 disjoint letters guarantees low recovery for every method. The map figure was specified (2026-05-25_shape-map-and-alphabet-decision.md) but never rendered. Value: honest reporting + a usable artifact.

Tier C — speculative, cheap, mechanistically distinct (weak priors)

C1Persistent homology / topological descriptors (ripser/gudhi, absent) — a jump is a sharp critical pair; cheap and novel, but the 50-pt ridge already encodes the jump as a large delta (M8 added the explicit derivative and still lost), so likely a noise-robust re-encoding, not new signal.

C2Functional PCA / elastic-fPCA (skfda, absent) — plain fPCA just re-bases the same Euclidean space (≈0.58–0.75); only the elastic variant raises the ceiling, and that collapses into A1. Fold it into A1.

5 · What was correctly killed — do not relitigate

  • The 2-D image-VAE / learned-encoder family is genuinely dead (M10 0.009 → B+A 0.105; Phase-0a frozen 0.517 vs random-init 0.501 is a sound balanced-accuracy falsifier with a control). Shape does not live in the 2-D pixel grid; recon/BCE burns capacity on pitch/duration; shift-aug gives only local ±15 kHz invariance.
  • 1-D learned contrastive is also dead, not "untried": M9 already ran on the registered ridge, no reconstruction, with elastic-warp augmentation — the exact "do it better" recipe — and scored 0.34.
  • Scale-normalized ridge (lab_shape_scalenorm) and raising max_jump_bins / a discontinuity channel were both tried; the jump bottleneck is label noise + class rarity, not the ridge cap.

6 · Next experiment — the labeled-subset result is in; scale it up

The "less likely, high-value" outcome actually happened on the labeled subset (§4·A1): elastic warp-alignment separates jump/complex that registration smears. The remaining work is to confirm it at scale and on a better anchor:

  1. Full-corpus soft-DTW k-means (tslearn, already installed; or fdasrsf kmeans_align for true SRVF geodesic) on the (67337, 50) ridges → produce a new shape alphabet, compare its human-anchored kNN-purity + NMI to models/shape_kmeans/k20.joblib, with a random-init control. If the jump/complex gain holds at scale, this becomes the new production preprocessor.
  2. Expand the human gold set FIRST — to the wild cohorts (5970/3452/9252, currently zero labels) and to ≥20 examples per shape family (only 4 true chevrons today). Every decimal here is throttled by that N.
  3. Make human-anchored kNN-purity (not η²) the standing gate, exactly as ROADMAP_SHAPE_INVARIANT_LATENT.md originally specified.

What is NOT worth re-running: anything in §5 (the 2-D VAE family, 1-D learned contrastive, scale-norm). Those are falsified.
