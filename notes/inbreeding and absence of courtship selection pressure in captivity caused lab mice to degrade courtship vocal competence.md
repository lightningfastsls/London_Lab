# Inbreeding and absence of courtship selection pressure in captivity caused lab mice to degrade courtship vocal competence

A century of inbreeding and the absence of natural selection pressure for courtship success in laboratory environments has caused lab mice (e.g., C57BL/6) to degrade or lose courtship behavioral competence. USVs are one marker of this: lab mice don't need to "work" for breeding in captivity, so the vocal courtship repertoire may have atrophied. The hypothesis is specifically directional — not just "wild and lab mice differ" but "lab mice have degraded relative to wild mice." This is more specific than the neutral framing in [[wild versus lab mouse USV comparison tests whether domestication altered vocal repertoires]], which asks whether differences exist. This note captures the specific causal mechanism (inbreeding + relaxed courtship selection) and the directional prediction (degradation, not just difference). Preliminary evidence supports this: since [[wild mice show more diverse USV repertoires than lab mice as preliminary evidence for courtship vocal degradation]].
