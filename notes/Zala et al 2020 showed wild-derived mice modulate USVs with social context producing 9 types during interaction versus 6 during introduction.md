# Zala et al 2020 showed wild-derived mice modulate USVs with social context producing 9 types during interaction versus 6 during introduction

Zala et al. (2020, *Frontiers in Zoology*) demonstrated that **wild-derived house mice modulate their USV repertoire based on social context**: they produce **9 syllable types during direct interaction** versus **6 during the introduction phase**. This context-dependent repertoire expansion shows that wild mice actively adjust their vocal behavior depending on the social situation.

This finding has several implications for our research:

1. **Repertoire flexibility**: Wild mice don't use a fixed syllable set -- they dynamically deploy different subsets of their repertoire. This is consistent with [[Chabout et al 2015 established that male mice change syllable syntax with social context]], extending syntax-level modulation to repertoire-level modulation.

2. **Experimental design**: Recording conditions matter. If we compare wild and lab mouse USVs, we need to control for social context, because repertoire differences might reflect context differences rather than population differences. This is critical for [[wild versus lab mouse USV comparison tests whether domestication altered vocal repertoires]].

3. **Population differences**: The 9/6 type split in wild mice provides a concrete datapoint for the expected range of syllable types in wild populations, contextualizing DeepSqueak's k=20 and MUPET's 100-140 types from lab recordings.

The 9 types during interaction suggests a richer vocal repertoire than the simplest taxonomies (Holy & Guo's ~10 types) might capture, while still being far fewer than data-driven methods find, supporting the view that [[forcing USVs into discrete categories may obscure the continuous variation that distinguishes populations]].
